                 Dead Letter Wake: Relay Continuity Memorandum


Introduction

This memorandum records the continuity procedure used after the research vessel Sable Meridian

stopped answering routine relay checks. The process preserves the last authenticated instruction

without depending on the vessel's failed uplink.


Background Information

At 02:30 UTC the shore relay accepted a numbered sequence from the vessel's watch account. Most

deliveries remained visible in the packet recorder, while one crossed an encrypted transport boundary

and was retained only in deferred queue state. The message series must be restored before its

contents can be trusted.


Objective

Recover the final wake authorization, verify that every numbered component belongs to the same

message identity, and preserve the resulting document as evidence. Any value copied from an

incomplete sequence is to be treated as a training artifact.


Strategy Overview

The recovery team will correlate wire traffic with the relay's queue journal, rebuild the original MIME

entity, and examine the attached authorization memorandum. The document export service applied a

mosaic filter before discarding its source text; the retained calibration capture is the only supported

reversal reference.
Execution Plan

Phase 1: Correlate Deliveries

Group partial messages by their MIME identity and number. Use the encrypted session's endpoint

and time to identify its deferred queue record rather than attempting to recover transport secrets.


Phase 2: Restore the Message

Join the seven raw partial bodies in sequence and parse the result as a new MIME message.

Preserve the attached PDF exactly; rendering or recompression changes the mosaic block averages.


Phase 3: Validate Continuity

Extract the document's raster objects losslessly. The PIX-8 target and its same-renderer calibration

capture use gamma-encoded RGB averages and must be handled as original-resolution images.


Conclusion

A successful recovery proves that the final instruction survived both the missing wire fragment and

the document export service's unsafe visual redaction.
